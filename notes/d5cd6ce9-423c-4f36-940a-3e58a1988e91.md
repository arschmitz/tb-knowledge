# Ingestion trial timing: preparation took seconds; semantic stage costs remain unresolved

The 2026-10-14 receipt audit found fast initial automatic preparation. It did not identify remote source reads as the cause of the slow trials. Existing receipts cannot reliably divide the remaining work into reading, reasoning, writing, review and repair.

## Observed compact-trial intervals

The [published compact report](5f97d574-30bd-4240-9001-c5b5445f087c.md) supplies the baseline and publication limits. Setup lasted 1,384.161 seconds (23.07 minutes). The active worker/publisher interval lasted 878.713 seconds (14.65 minutes). Later audit, correction and reporting lasted 837.874 seconds (13.96 minutes). The full job was 3,100.737 seconds (51.68 minutes). These three intervals are disjoint.

Initial preparation took 8,717.769 ms, including 229 successful blob reads, 230 source/context origins and 1,215,216 source bytes. Its inner packet-preparation receipt ran from 22:55:54.218 to 22:56:02.801 UTC and measured 8,582.451 ms. The outer and inner numbers describe overlapping work and must not be added.

The attempt intervals total 8.027959 seconds. They exclude diagnosis and repairs between attempts, which occurred inside the overall active window. Preparation and publisher time also sit inside that window; they are not additional full-job costs.

The earlier grouped trial prepared 47 blobs and 184,076 bytes in about 1.227 seconds. The [capacity calibration](acf9ed87-1c83-4bcf-b603-b9c3b483d2f6.md) prepared 51 blobs and 335,088 bytes in 1.634 seconds cold and 0.540 seconds warm. These are initial input-preparation measurements, not full source-read totals or behavior-analysis rates.

## What the tool actually times

[prepareWorkloadPackets](https://github.com/arschmitz/tb-tools/blob/68ebea8b5c85d3dab6d483b023bd34e23bd509dc/commands/knowledge/ingestion-workload.mjs#L147-L241) uses `performance.now()` for automatic preparation. That interval includes validation, local Git/cache reads, packet construction, searches and writes. It is not pure disk input/output time.

[submitGroupedResponse](https://github.com/arschmitz/tb-tools/blob/68ebea8b5c85d3dab6d483b023bd34e23bd509dc/commands/knowledge/ingestion-grouped.mjs#L204-L224) instead calculates `elapsedMs` from producer-supplied `startedAt` and `finishedAt`. It validates the date syntax/order, then saves `end - start`. It does not measure continuous model activity. Some worker start timestamps were later disclosed as guesses; others timed response submission only. They cannot establish a reading-versus-reasoning-versus-writing cost breakdown.

Initial packet source access uses pinned local Git objects. Git publication fetch/push is a separate action. Later analysis and review reads were not recorded in a complete transport ledger. The receipts therefore neither show remote source access as the bottleneck nor bound every later local read.

## Outcome and limits

The compact trial retained 18 reviewed origins without its known omitted-test exception and zero verified full-process timing credits. All 86 raw records were found remotely afterward through a route outside the benchmark publisher receipts. Zero verified credit does not mean zero physical publication. The [resource2 correction](0aa389c4-bb65-490f-8881-d88a77f4a36e.md) preserves the omitted direct test callers.

Measured initial preparation was about 0.97% of the fixed 900-second compact window. Removing only that measured cost cannot yield a ninefold improvement. Later source-input costs, model-stage costs and repair time remain insufficiently separated. No population-wide completion forecast follows from these small, selected trials.

No new product test, network read, live-store import or repository change ran during the timing audit. Runner-owned timestamps and separate source-read, response, review, repair and publication receipts are proposed instrumentation, not a new policy or already-completed tool feature.
